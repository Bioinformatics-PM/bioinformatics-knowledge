# Scientific Literature

> [!abstract]
> How scientific knowledge is recorded and communicated: finding, reading and critically appraising papers, how peer review, preprints and open access work, how evidence is synthesized, and how to write papers, talks and grant proposals.

## Why it matters for bioinformatics

Every method, database and reference result a bioinformatician uses is described in a paper, and every analysis ends as a figure, a report or a manuscript. Reading fast and critically, and writing clearly, are core skills: MIT requires communication-intensive subjects in both its biology and its computer science and molecular biology majors, and research programs end with a written thesis.[^mit7][^mit67][^sjtu]

## Learning path

### Stage 1 - Foundations (L1)

1. [[Primary Literature]] (L1): distinguish primary research articles from reviews, textbooks and popular sources.
2. [[Scientific Paper]] (L1): know the anatomy of a paper (title, abstract, IMRaD sections, figures, supplementary material, data availability).
3. [[Review Article]] (L1): use narrative reviews to enter a field and know their bias toward the authors' view.
4. [[Literature Search]] (L1): search PubMed, Europe PMC and Google Scholar with keywords, Boolean operators and citation chaining.[^pubmed]
5. [[Reading a Scientific Paper]] (L1): read in passes (abstract and figures first, methods last) and summarize the claim and its evidence.
6. [[Reference Management]] (L1): store references with DOIs in a manager such as Zotero and cite them consistently.

### Stage 2 - Core (L2)

7. [[Medical Subject Headings]] (L2): search with MeSH, the controlled vocabulary that indexes MEDLINE.[^pubmed]
8. [[Peer Review]] (L2): explain single-blind, double-blind and open review, what reviewers check, how to write a review, and what peer review cannot guarantee.[^sciwrite]
9. [[Preprint]] (L2): use bioRxiv, medRxiv and arXiv preprints knowing they are not yet peer reviewed.
10. [[Open Access]] (L2): distinguish gold, green and diamond open access, and find legal free copies (PubMed Central, repositories).
11. [[Bibliometrics]] (L2): read impact factors, citation counts and the h-index with their known limits (DORA).
12. [[Predatory Journal]] (L2): recognize journals that charge fees without real peer review.[^sciwrite]
13. [[Critical Appraisal]] (L2): judge whether a paper's design, statistics and data support its conclusion.
14. [[Scientific Writing]] (L2): write clear, concise sentences, paragraphs and sections, with one message per paragraph and figure.[^sciwrite]
15. [[Scientific Presentation]] (L2): build a talk or poster around one message and figures readable from a distance.
16. [[Science Communication]] (L2): explain a result to a general audience or a journalist without overstating it.[^sciwrite]

### Stage 3 - Advanced (L3)

17. [[Publication Process]] (L3): follow a manuscript from journal choice and cover letter to review, response to reviewers and proofs.[^sciwrite]
18. [[Retraction]] (L3): know how errors and misconduct are corrected (errata, expressions of concern, retractions) and check a paper's status.
19. [[Systematic Review]] (L3): run a reproducible search and selection with a PRISMA flow diagram.
20. [[Meta-Analysis]] (L3): combine effect sizes across studies with fixed- and random-effects models and read a forest plot.
21. [[Grant Writing]] (L3): write the aims, significance and approach of a proposal for a funder, and read its evaluation criteria.[^sciwrite]

> [!tip]
> From Stage 3 onward, follow each major topic to its landmark paper (see [[Curriculum#From concept to paper]]). Items 5 and 13 improve only with volume: one paper a week is enough.
