# Carnegie Mellon University - BS Computational Biology

> [!abstract]
> A computational biology bachelor housed in a School of Computer Science: a CS major's theory and algorithms core, a mathematics and statistics core, a four-course biology core, and a dedicated computational biology core that starts in the first year.

## Why this source

The only program in the benchmark that introduces computational biology *as a subject* in the first year (Great Ideas in Computational Biology) and then requires both [[Genomics|computational genomics]] and [[Systems Biology|biological modeling]]. It shows a complete, balanced core list with units, which makes it the best model for weighting the vault's domains.

Verified first-year placement: 03-151 Honors Modern Biology in the first fall, Great Ideas in Computational Biology (02-251) in the first spring; the rest of the first-year schedule follows the CS major's sample sequence.

## How to use it

- Copy its **balance**: roughly equal weight for mathematics and statistics, computer science, and biology, with chemistry and physics reduced to one course each.
- Copy its **early hook**: a survey course of computational biology ideas in year 1, before the algorithms that justify them.
- Use 02-510 and 02-512 as the L3 endpoints of the [[Genomics]] and [[Systems Biology]] syllabi.

## Caveats

- Course numbering changed over time: the first-year survey appears both as 02-251 (12 units) and as 02-180/02-181; check the current catalog.
- Unit totals for the CS and computational biology cores were not verified consistently; only the per-course units shown above are reliable.
- No organic chemistry requirement was found in the verified material; it may exist as an elective or pre-med recommendation.
