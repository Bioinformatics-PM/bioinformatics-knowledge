# Stanford University - BS Biomedical Computation

> [!abstract]
> Stanford's Biomedical Computation (BMC) major: an interdepartmental Bachelor of Science in Engineering that builds a common core in mathematics, statistics, computer science, biology and chemistry, then splits into four tracks (Informatics, Simulation, Cellular/Molecular, Organs/Organ Systems) and requires directed research.

## Why this source

It shows the **track model**: one shared quantitative and biological core, then a choice between data-driven (Informatics), model-driven (Simulation) and biology-driven tracks. The vault follows the same logic: a common L1 and L2, then an L3 that can lean toward [[Sequence Analysis]] and [[Genomics]] (informatics) or [[Systems Biology]] (simulation).

Requirements from the Stanford Engineering undergraduate handbook (2026-27 edition at time of verification). No semester plan was verified.

| Research | 6 units of directed research with a Stanford faculty member | [[Scientific Method]] |

## How to use it

- Use the mathematics block as a minimum: calculus, discrete mathematics and one probability course before any track.
- Use the track split to organize the vault's L3 electives rather than forcing every learner through both genomics and modeling.
- The research requirement supports the vault's Lab projects as the equivalent of directed research.

## Caveats

- Only the blocks above were verified. Chemistry and physics requirements, the titles of BIO 82 to 86 and the track course lists were not.
- The degree is a BS in Engineering, not a biology degree; School of Engineering requirements outside the major were not verified.
