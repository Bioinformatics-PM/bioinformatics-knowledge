# University of Cambridge - Natural Sciences Tripos

> [!abstract]
> Cambridge's Natural Sciences degree: a broad first year (three experimental sciences plus one mathematics course), a narrower second year, and a specialized third year, through which biologists reach molecular biology, biochemistry or genetics.

## Why this source

It is the model of **breadth first, specialization late**, and it makes mathematics mandatory in year 1 for every scientist. Its first-year Mathematical Biology option is the best example in the benchmark of teaching [[Probability]], [[Statistical Inference]], modeling and programming *to biologists, with biological data*, from day one.

## Coverage

| Year (Part) | Structure | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 (Part IA) | Three experimental subjects from: Biology of Cells, Chemistry, Earth Sciences, Evolution and Behaviour, Materials Science, Physics, Physiology of Organisms; plus one mathematics course: Mathematics A, Mathematics B or Mathematical Biology | [[Cell Biology]], [[General Chemistry]], [[Evolution]], [[Physics]], [[Physiology]] |
| Year 1 Mathematical Biology | algebra, linear and non-linear differential equations, mathematical modeling, matrix algebra, probability and statistical methods; practicals in R on real biological data, including simple programming, data handling, statistical testing and bioinformatics | [[Calculus]], [[Differential Equations]], [[Linear Algebra]], [[Probability]], [[Statistical Inference]], [[Programming]] |
| Year 2 (Part IB) | Subjects include Biochemistry and Molecular Biology (genome to RNA and protein, regulation, protein structure and function), Cell and Developmental Biology, Animal Biology, Ecology, Pharmacology, Chemistry A and B, Physics A and B, Mathematics | [[Biochemistry]], [[Molecular Biology]], [[Protein Structure]], [[Cell Biology]], [[Developmental Biology]] |
| Year 3 (Part II) | single-subject specialization (e.g. Biochemistry) | [[Biochemistry]] |

## How to use it

- Borrow the rule that a quantitative course is compulsory in L1, and that it is taught on biological examples.
- Use the Part IA Mathematical Biology syllabus as a checklist for the vault's L1 mathematics and statistics: differential equations, matrices, probability, statistical tests, and R or Python on real data.
- Note the order: protein structure and gene regulation arrive in year 2, after the broad cell biology of year 1.

## Caveats

- Students choose subjects each year, so there is no single "biology" program; the table shows the options relevant to a biologist.
- The first-year mathematics page for biologists is published under a "qbiol" path, which suggests the course may be renamed (e.g. Quantitative Biology); check the current name.
- Part II options for genetics or systems biology were not verified here.
