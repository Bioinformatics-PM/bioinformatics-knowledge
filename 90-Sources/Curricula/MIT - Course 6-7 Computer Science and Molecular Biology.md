# MIT - Course 6-7 Computer Science and Molecular Biology

> [!abstract]
> MIT's joint Bachelor of Science in Computer Science and Molecular Biology: a full computer science core (programming, discrete mathematics, algorithms) welded to the molecular biology core (genetics, biochemistry, cell biology), with computational biology as a restricted elective.

## Why this source

The clearest example of a program designed *from both ends*: it does not add a few coding classes to a biology degree, it requires the same algorithms foundation as a CS major and the same genetics, biochemistry and cell biology as a biology major. It shows that a serious bioinformatics curriculum needs [[Algorithms]] and [[Linear Algebra]] as firmly as [[Genetics]].

## Coverage

The degree chart lists requirements, not a year-by-year plan. Only the General Institute Requirements (GIR) science core is placed early: MIT students "begin with" these six subjects.

| Block | Subjects (MIT numbers, units) | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| GIR science core (first) | Calculus 18.01, 18.02; Physics 8.01, 8.02; Chemistry 5.111, 5.112 or 3.091; Biology 7.012, 7.014, 7.015 or 7.016 | [[Calculus]], [[Physics]], [[General Chemistry]], [[Cell Biology]], [[Molecular Biology]] |
| Math and intro CS | 6.100A Introduction to Computer Science Programming in Python (6); 6.1200[J] Mathematics for Computer Science (12); 6.C06[J] Linear Algebra and Optimization (12) | [[Programming]], [[Discrete Mathematics]], [[Linear Algebra]] |
| Chemistry | 5.12 Organic Chemistry I (12); 5.601 Thermodynamics I (6) | [[Organic Chemistry]], [[Thermodynamics]] |
| Intro laboratory | 7.002 and 7.003[J] (Fundamentals of Experimental Molecular Biology; Applied Molecular Biology Laboratory) or 20.109 (15 to 18) | [[Molecular Biology]] |
| CS foundation (3 subjects) | includes 6.1010 Fundamentals of Programming (12) and 6.1210 Introduction to Algorithms | [[Programming]], [[Algorithms]] |
| Biology foundation (3 subjects) | 7.03 Genetics; 7.05 General Biochemistry (or 5.07[J]); 7.06 Cell Biology | [[Genetics]], [[Biochemistry]], [[Cell Biology]] |
| Computational biology elective | e.g. 6.8701[J] Computational Biology: Genomes, Networks, Evolution (12) | [[Sequence Analysis]], [[Genomics]], [[Phylogenetics]], [[Systems Biology]] |
| Biology restricted electives | e.g. 7.08[J] Fundamentals of Chemical Biology; 7.093 Modern Biostatistics with 7.094 Modern Computational Biology | [[Statistical Inference]] |
| Communication | at least two communication-intensive (CI-M) subjects in the major | [[Scientific Writing]] |

GIRs also include 8 HASS subjects, 2 REST subjects and a laboratory requirement (17 GIR subjects in all).

## How to use it

- Use it as the **upper bound** for the computer science side of the vault: if a topic is in 6.1210, it belongs in the [[Algorithms]] syllabus.
- Pair it with [[MIT - Course 7 Biology]] to see what a CS-heavy program drops from pure biology: 5.602 (second half of thermodynamics and kinetics) and 7.19 Communication in Experimental Biology, while genetics, biochemistry, cell biology and organic chemistry stay.
- Treat 6.8701 as the model of the first "real" computational biology course: it comes after algorithms and the biology foundation, not before.

## Caveats

- The chart gives no semester placement beyond the GIRs; [[Curriculum Benchmark]] marks the other subjects as "required, year not published".
- MIT subject numbers change over time; older course materials may use earlier numbers for the same subjects.
- Only the subjects named above were verified; the full elective lists and the unit totals of the major were not.
