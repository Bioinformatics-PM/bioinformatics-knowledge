# Introduction to Linear Algebra (Strang)

> [!abstract]
> Gilbert Strang's applied linear algebra textbook, 6th edition (2023), the text of MIT 18.06, ending with optimization and learning from data.

## Why this source

The standard computational and geometric first course, from the author who has taught linear algebra at MIT for more than 50 years. Key ideas are expressed as matrix factorizations, the form in which they appear in data analysis code.

## Coverage

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Vectors, independent columns, rank and column space ("early vector spaces") | [[Vector]], [[Matrix]], [[Matrix Rank\|Rank]] |
| Linear equations | Elimination, fundamental subspaces | [[System of Linear Equations]], [[Gaussian Elimination]] |
| Least squares | Projections, fitting | [[Least Squares]], [[Linear Regression]] |
| Eigenvalues and singular values | Diagonalization, SVD | [[Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors]], [[Singular Value Decomposition]] |
| Final chapters | Optimization and learning from data | [[Gradient Descent]], [[Principal Component Analysis]] |

## How to use it

- L1: first chapters through fundamental subspaces, with MIT 18.06 lectures.
- L2: least squares, eigenvalues and SVD; then the learning-from-data chapters.
- Follow with [[Linear Algebra Done Right (Axler)]] for a proof-based view.

## Caveats

- Paid (the book site hosts sample material). The 5th edition started from linear equations; the 6th starts from vector spaces and has new chapters on optimization and learning from data.
- Chapter titles not verified here; coverage from the publisher description.
