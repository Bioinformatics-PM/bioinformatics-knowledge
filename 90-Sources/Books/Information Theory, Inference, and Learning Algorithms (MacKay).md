# Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms (MacKay)

> [!abstract]
> A textbook that unifies information theory, Bayesian inference and machine learning, free to read on screen from the author's website.

## Why this source

MacKay treats entropy, coding, inference and learning as one subject, with a physicist's intuition and more than 400 exercises, some with worked solutions. For bioinformatics, it explains the quantities behind sequence logos, motif information content and mutual information between positions, and links them to probabilistic modeling. Cambridge University Press permits on-screen viewing of the PDF (printing is not permitted).

## How to use it

- L2: read the early chapters on probability, entropy and inference for items 28 to 30 of [[Probability]]; do the exercises marked as easy.
- L3: return to the Monte Carlo and model comparison chapters alongside [[Bayesian Data Analysis (Gelman)]].

## Caveats

- Idiosyncratic style and order; some topics (error-correcting codes) are far from biology.
- Free version is for on-screen viewing only; the print book is paid.
