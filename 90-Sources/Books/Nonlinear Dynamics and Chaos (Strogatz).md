# Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos (Strogatz)

> [!abstract]
> The classic applied introduction to nonlinear dynamics and chaos, with applications to physics, biology, chemistry and engineering; 3rd edition (2024).

## Why this source

Stresses analytical methods, concrete examples and geometric intuition. It is the foundation for dynamical models in biology (gene circuits, epidemics, oscillators) that systems biology builds on.

## Coverage

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| One-dimensional flows | First-order differential equations and their bifurcations | [[Ordinary Differential Equation]], [[Fixed Point]], [[Bifurcation]] |
| Two-dimensional flows | Phase plane analysis, limit cycles and their bifurcations | [[Phase Portrait\|Phase Plane]], [[Limit Cycle]] |
| Chaos | Lorenz equations, iterated maps, period doubling, renormalization, fractals, strange attractors | [[Differential Equations\|Chaos]], [[Differential Equations\|Strange Attractor]] |
| Ch. 13 (new in 3rd ed.) | The Kuramoto model of coupled oscillators | [[Differential Equations\|Synchronization]] |
| Biological examples | Updated SIR epidemic model, Selkov model of glycolytic oscillations | [[SIR Model]], [[Dynamical System]] |

## How to use it

- L2: one- and two-dimensional flows, after calculus and a first linear algebra course.
- L3: chaos chapters and the biological examples; model gene-circuit notes on the phase-plane method.

## Caveats

- Paid. Prerequisites: multivariable calculus, linear algebra, a first physics course.
- Only chapter 13 was verified by number.
