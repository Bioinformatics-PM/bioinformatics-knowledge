# An Introduction to Genetic Analysis (Griffiths)

> [!abstract]
> The reference genetics textbook built around genetic analysis and problem solving; the 7th edition is free on NCBI Bookshelf.

## Why this source

Genetics taught as a way of reasoning from crosses, mutants and data, with many quantitative problems. It is the best free anchor for transmission genetics, the bridge to [[Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium|population genetics]], and the logic behind gene mapping, all of which bioinformatics later automates.

## Coverage

The textbook divides genetics into transmission, molecular and evolutionary genetics. Chapter-level detail not verified here.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Transmission genetics | Mendelian inheritance, chromosomes, linkage and mapping | [[Mendelian Inheritance]], [[Meiosis]], [[Genetic Linkage]], [[Genetic Recombination\|Recombination]] |
| Molecular genetics | DNA structure and replication, gene function, gene expression | [[DNA]], [[DNA Replication]], [[Gene]], [[Transcription]], [[Translation]] |
| Mutation and genomes | Mutation, repair, recombinant DNA, genomics | [[Mutation]], [[DNA Repair]], [[Molecular Cloning\|Recombinant DNA]], [[Genome]] |
| Evolutionary genetics | Population and quantitative genetics | [[Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium]], [[Genetic Drift]], [[Natural Selection]] |

## How to use it

- L1: transmission genetics first, and do the problems.
- L2: molecular genetics chapters, then the population genetics part as a lead-in to [[Principles of Population Genetics (Hartl)]].

## Caveats

- Year: NCBI and most catalogs give 2000 (W. H. Freeman); some list 1999.
- The current edition is the 12th (Griffiths, Doebley, Peichel, Wassarman); genomics content in the 7th edition is dated.
- NCBI Bookshelf may only allow search, not browsing, for this title. Chapter titles not verified in this pass.
