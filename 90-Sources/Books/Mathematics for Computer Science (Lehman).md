# Mathematics for Computer Science (Lehman)

> [!abstract]
> The free MIT textbook for 6.042J: proofs, discrete structures and discrete probability for computer scientists.

## Why this source

The best free discrete-mathematics text: rigorous, readable, with many problems, and written for computing. It teaches how to write proofs, which the rest of the math track assumes.

## Coverage

Coverage divides roughly into thirds.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Proofs | Definitions, proofs, induction, sets, functions, relations | [[Mathematical Proof]], [[Mathematical Induction]], [[Set]], [[Binary Relation\|Relation]] |
| Structures | Number theory and modular arithmetic, graphs, state machines, counting | [[Modular Arithmetic]], [[Graph\|Graph Theory]], [[Combinatorics]] |
| Probability | Discrete probability, conditional probability, random variables, expectation | [[Probability]], [[Conditional Probability]], [[Random Variable]], [[Expected Value]] |

## How to use it

- L1: the proofs part first, then graphs and counting (needed for algorithms and [[Graph Traversal|graph algorithms]]).
- L2: the probability part, before [[Introduction to Probability (Blitzstein)]].
- Use with the MIT OCW 6.042J lectures and problem sets.

## Caveats

- Several revisions circulate (the OCW course pages host 2010 and 2015 versions; a 2017 revision is widely mirrored). Check the date on the title page and cite that revision.
- Chapter numbers differ between revisions; cite by section title.
