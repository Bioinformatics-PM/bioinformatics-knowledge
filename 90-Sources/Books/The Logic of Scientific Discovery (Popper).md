# The Logic of Scientific Discovery (Popper)

> [!abstract]
> Karl Popper's classic of the philosophy of science, which proposes falsifiability as the criterion that separates empirical science from non-science.

## Why this source

It is the primary source for falsificationism: a theory is scientific if it forbids some possible observations, so that it can be refuted, and theories are never verified by accumulating confirmations, only corroborated by surviving tests. Most introductions to the scientific method, including the vault's, rest on its ideas, and reading the original shows what Popper actually claimed and what he did not.

## How to use it

- L1: read a secondary summary first, then the opening part on the problem of induction and the criterion of falsifiability, for items 4 and 5 of [[Scientific Method]].
- L2: return to the parts on testing and corroboration for item 10, and contrast them with [[Platt 1964 - Strong Inference]].

## Caveats

- Dense philosophy with long technical appendices (probability, corroboration); most readers need only the first part.
- Later work (Kuhn, Lakatos) criticized strict falsificationism as a description of how science works; see [[Paradigm Shift]].
