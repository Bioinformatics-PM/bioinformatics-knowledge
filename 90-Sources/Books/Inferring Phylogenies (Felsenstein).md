# Inferring Phylogenies (Felsenstein)

> [!abstract]
> The reference on the assumptions and logic of phylogenetic inference, by the author of PHYLIP and of maximum-likelihood phylogenetics.

## Why this source

Explains how phylogenies are reconstructed and used to infer evolutionary processes, across parsimony, distance, likelihood and Bayesian methods. Written by the researcher often called the father of statistical phylogenetics.

## Coverage

35 chapters; data types covered include discretely coded characters, molecular sequences, gene frequencies and quantitative traits (also restriction sites, RAPDs, microsatellites). Chapter titles not listed here.

## How to use it

- L3: read the likelihood and models material after [[Felsenstein 1981 - Evolutionary Trees from DNA Sequences]].
- M1: Bayesian, testing and comparative-method chapters as needed for projects.

## Caveats

- Paid. Intended for graduate courses; assumes statistics, calculus and basic matrix algebra.
- Dated 2004 in most catalogs, 2003 in some reviews.
- Part grouping above is by method and was not checked against the chapter list.
