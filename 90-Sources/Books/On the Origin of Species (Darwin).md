# On the Origin of Species (Darwin)

> [!abstract]
> Darwin's 1859 book, *On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection*, which argued that species descend with modification from common ancestors and proposed natural selection as the main cause of that modification.

## Why this source

The founding text of evolutionary biology, cited in the vault for the historical form of two claims: [[Common Descent]] (descent with modification, illustrated by the book's single figure, a branching diagram of diverging lineages) and [[Natural Selection]] (the argument from variation, heredity and the struggle for existence). It is a primary historical source: read a modern text for current evidence and mechanisms.

## Coverage

Published in London by John Murray on 24 November 1859; first printing of 1,250 copies; standard bibliographic number Freeman 373. The first edition contains one folding lithographed diagram (facing p. 117).

## How to use it

- L1: read the closing recapitulation and the chapter on natural selection for the structure of the argument, after [[Biology 2e (OpenStax)]] ch. 18.
- L2: the chapters on classification and on the geological record show how Darwin weighed evidence before genetics existed.
- The full text of every edition is free at Darwin Online.

## Caveats

- Darwin lacked a correct mechanism of heredity: Mendelian genetics and the population-genetic formalization of selection came later ([[Principles of Population Genetics (Hartl)]]).
- Darwin revised the text in later editions; the vault cites the first edition. Chapter numbers and titles are not cited in notes because they were not verified in this pass.
- Bibliographic data (publisher, date, print run, Freeman number, folding diagram) verified against Darwin Online and rare-book catalog descriptions.
