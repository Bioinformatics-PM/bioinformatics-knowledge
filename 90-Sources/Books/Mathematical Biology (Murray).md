# Mathematical Biology (Murray)

> [!abstract]
> *Mathematical Biology I: An Introduction*, the classic reference for modeling biological systems, mainly with ordinary differential equations; 3rd edition (2002).

## Why this source

A sweeping classic of the field, volume 17 of Springer's Interdisciplinary Applied Mathematics series. It shows how to turn a biological question into a model and analyze it, which complements the more general [[Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos (Strogatz)]].

## Coverage

Volume I uses mainly ordinary differential equations and suits undergraduate and graduate courses. Chapter list not verified here.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Population models | Continuous and discrete population dynamics | [[Mathematical Modeling\|Population Dynamics]], [[Ordinary Differential Equation]] |
| Biochemical and biological oscillators | Reaction kinetics and oscillations | [[Michaelis-Menten Kinetics\|Enzyme Kinetics]], [[Limit Cycle]] |
| Epidemics and other applications | Infectious disease models; 3rd-edition applications include temperature-dependent sex determination and marital interaction models | [[SIR Model]] |

## How to use it

- L3: after Strogatz's one- and two-dimensional flows, pick chapters matching a Lab project.
- M1: use as a reference when building a model; the companion Volume II is not covered by this note.

## Caveats

- Paid. Dense; assumes comfort with ODEs.
- Part grouping is by topic and was only partly verified (ODE focus and the new 3rd-edition applications are verified).
