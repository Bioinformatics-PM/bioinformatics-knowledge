# NHGRI DNA Sequencing Costs

> [!abstract]
> NHGRI's dataset and charts of the cost of DNA sequencing at the sequencing centers it funds, tracked since 2001 as cost per megabase and cost per human-sized genome.

## Why this source

It is the standard source for the famous curve showing sequencing costs falling far faster than Moore's law would predict. NHGRI tracks the costs at its Genome Sequencing Program centers as a benchmark of technological progress and of the program's capacity. The data are summarized with two metrics, the cost of determining one megabase of sequence of specified quality and the cost of sequencing a human-sized genome, and can be downloaded.

## How to use it

- L3: download the data, plot both metrics on a log scale, and fit a halving time before and after 2008; set it against [[Eroom's Law]] from [[Scannell 2012 - Diagnosing the Decline in Pharmaceutical R&D Efficiency]].
- Use it to answer item 13 of [[Innovation and Entrepreneurship]]: what cheap sequencing changed, and what it did not (interpretation, storage, clinical validation).

## Caveats

- Production costs at NHGRI-funded centers only, following their accounting rules; not the price a customer pays, and not the full cost of analysis, storage or interpretation.
- Updates are irregular; check the date of the last data point.
- Verified in this pass: page URL, the two metrics, the 2001 start and the program they describe; the exact cost values were not copied here.
