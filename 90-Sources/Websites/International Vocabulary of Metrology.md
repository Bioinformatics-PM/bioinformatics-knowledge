# International Vocabulary of Metrology

> [!abstract]
> The international reference dictionary of measurement terms (VIM), maintained by the Joint Committee for Guides in Metrology and published online by the BIPM, one numbered entry per concept.

## Why this source

Textbooks use "accuracy", "precision", "error" and "bias" loosely, and sometimes contradict each other. The VIM is the definition that metrology institutes, standards bodies and instrument makers share, so it settles what each word means. The online version adds informative annotations under each entry.

## Coverage

Clause 2 ("Measurement") defines the vocabulary of measurement quality.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Clause 2, measurement quality | Measurement error; systematic and random measurement error; measurement bias; measurement accuracy, trueness (entry 2.14) and precision; repeatability and reproducibility conditions | [[Measurement Error]] |

## How to use it

- L1: read the entries for trueness and precision and their notes, then the figure-free definitions of systematic and random error; compare with [[Measurement Error]].
- L2: use the repeatability and reproducibility entries when reading assay validation or method-comparison papers.

## Caveats

- Definitions are terse and written for metrologists; read them with a worked example.
- The VIM's "reproducibility" (precision across laboratories, operators and instruments) is not the computational meaning used in [[Reproducibility]].
- Verified in this pass: the online VIM3 site (jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en), the edition (JCGM 200:2012), the entry "measurement trueness" (2.14) and the wording of the definitions of trueness and precision. Other entry numbers were not checked: cite entries by their term.
