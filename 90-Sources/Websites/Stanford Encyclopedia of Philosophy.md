# Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

> [!abstract]
> A free, peer-reviewed online encyclopedia of philosophy maintained at Stanford University, each entry written and revised by a specialist; used here for its entry "The Problem of Induction".

## Why this source

Philosophy of science is easy to caricature. The encyclopedia gives authoritative, regularly revised entries with full bibliographies, at a level an undergraduate can read. The entry on the problem of induction, by Leah Henderson, states Hume's argument precisely and surveys the responses to it, which is the background a scientist needs before saying that data "prove" a general claim.

## How to use it

- L1: read the opening sections of "The Problem of Induction" for Hume's argument, after [[Inductive Reasoning#Core (L1)]].
- L2: read the sections on responses alongside [[The Logic of Scientific Discovery (Popper)]], whose answer is to give up inductive justification altogether.

## Caveats

- Entries are revised over time: cite the archived edition consulted (here Winter 2022, edited by Edward N. Zalta and Uri Nodelman) when a precise wording matters.
- Written by and for philosophers; the later sections of the entry are technical.
