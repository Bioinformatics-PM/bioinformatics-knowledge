# MIT 6.041SC - Probabilistic Systems Analysis and Applied Probability

> [!abstract]
> MIT's OCW Scholar probability course (Fall 2013) by John Tsitsiklis: modeling, quantification and analysis of uncertainty, built for independent study.

## Why this source

- Named prerequisite of [[MIT 6.047 - Computational Biology]].
- Exceptionally complete: lecture videos, lecture slides, readings, recitation problems with solutions, tutorial problems with solutions, problem sets with solutions and exams with solutions.
- Follows Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis's textbook *Introduction to Probability*.

## Coverage

Standard 6.041 topic sequence; see Caveats.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Probability models | Sample spaces, conditioning, Bayes' rule, independence, counting | [[Probability]], [[Conditional Probability]], [[Bayes' Theorem]] |
| Random variables | Discrete and continuous random variables, expectation, variance, joint distributions | [[Random Variable]], [[Expected Value]], [[Variance]], [[Probability Distribution]] |
| Random processes | Bernoulli and Poisson processes, Markov chains | [[Poisson Process]], [[Markov Chain]] |
| Limit theorems | Probability bounds, law of large numbers, central limit theorem | [[Law of Large Numbers]], [[Central Limit Theorem]] |
| Inference | Bayesian and classical statistical inference | [[Bayesian Inference]], [[Hypothesis Testing]] |

## How to use it

- **Level L2**, after [[MIT 18.05 - Introduction to Probability and Statistics]] or directly after [[MIT 18.02SC - Multivariable Calculus]].
- Per lecture: video, then recitation and tutorial problems, then the problem set. Markov chains are essential before [[Hidden Markov Model]].
- Alternative with a different style: [[Harvard Stat 110 - Probability]].

## Caveats

- Verified here: instructor, resource types, independent-study design, and a recitation on probability bounds. The topic table follows the known 6.041 syllabus and textbook structure, not a checked lecture list.
