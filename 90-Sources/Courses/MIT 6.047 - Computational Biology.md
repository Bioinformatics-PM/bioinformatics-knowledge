# MIT 6.047 - Computational Biology

> [!abstract]
> Manolis Kellis's MIT course (Fall 2015, also 6.878 and HST.507) on the algorithmic and machine learning foundations of computational biology, organized in three parts: genomes, networks, evolution.

## Why this source

- Combines foundations and research frontiers, working with large-scale biological datasets.
- Comes with an open textbook, *Computational Biology: Genomes, Networks, Evolution*, compiled by students over many years (PDF on OCW), plus lecture slides and problem sets.

## Coverage

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Genomes | Biological sequence analysis, sequence alignment, hashing, hidden Markov models, gene finding, comparative genomics, RNA structure | [[Sequence Alignment]], [[Dynamic Programming]], [[Hash Table]], [[Hidden Markov Model]], [[Gene Finding]], [[Comparative Genomics]], [[RNA Secondary Structure]] |
| Networks | Gene expression, clustering and classification, EM and Gibbs sampling, motifs, Bayesian networks, microRNAs, regulatory genomics, epigenomics | [[Gene Expression]], [[Clustering]], [[Expectation-Maximization Algorithm]], [[Gibbs Sampling]], [[Motif Finding]], [[Bayesian Network]], [[MicroRNA]], [[Epigenomics]] |
| Evolution | Gene and species trees, phylogenomics, coalescent, personal and population genomics, human ancestry, recent selection, disease mapping | [[Phylogenetic Tree]], [[Phylogenomics]], [[Coalescent Theory]], [[Population Genomics]], [[Genome-Wide Association Study]] |

## How to use it

- **Level L3 to M1**, the capstone of the computational biology track. Stated prerequisites: [[MIT 6.006 - Introduction to Algorithms]], [[MIT 6.041SC - Probabilistic Systems Analysis and Applied Probability]] and [[MIT 7.01SC - Fundamentals of Biology]].
- Read the textbook chapter, then the slides, then the problem set. Do the Genomes part first; pair it with [[Coursera UCSD - Bioinformatics Specialization]] for implementation practice.

## Caveats

- Grading was problem sets 30%, quiz 20%, final project 40%, scribing 10%: a project course, so self-study must replace the project with a Lab project.
- Lecture videos for this run were not confirmed on OCW. The open textbook is student-scribed and uneven; cite primary sources for claims.
