# Coursera Stanford - Writing in the Sciences

> [!abstract]
> Kristin Sainani's Stanford course teaching scientists to write more effectively: principles of clear writing, the structure of a scientific manuscript, publication and peer review, and writing for other audiences.

## Why this source

- Highly rated and widely followed (over 600,000 learners on Coursera), practical, with examples and exercises.
- Covers the whole publication process, including ethics and peer review, not just style.

## Coverage

8 modules, 9 assignments.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Principles of good writing | Clear, concise sentences; writing faster and with less anxiety | [[Scientific Writing]] |
| The manuscript | Tables and figures; results, methods, introduction and discussion sections | [[Scientific Paper]] |
| Publication | Peer review, ethical issues in publishing, plagiarism, authorship, submission, writing a peer review, predatory journals | [[Peer Review]], [[Research Ethics]], [[Plagiarism]], [[Authorship]], [[Predatory Journal]] |
| Other genres | Review papers, grants, letters of recommendation, personal essays | [[Review Article]], [[Grant Writing]] |
| Science communication | Working with the media, interviews, writing for general audiences | [[Science Communication]] |

## How to use it

- **Level L2 to L3**, before the first project report or internship thesis; revisit the publication module at M1.
- Apply each module to a real text: rewrite a Lab project README or report after the style modules.

## Caveats

- Auditing is free; graded assignments and the certificate follow Coursera's paid model. An earlier self-paced version ran on Stanford's own platform.
- `year` is the version currently offered.
