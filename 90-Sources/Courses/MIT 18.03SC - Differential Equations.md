# MIT 18.03SC - Differential Equations

> [!abstract]
> MIT's OCW Scholar course (Fall 2011) on ordinary differential equations, focused on driven first- and second-order constant-coefficient linear equations and 2x2 systems, with Arthur Mattuck's lectures.

## Why this source

- Built for independent study: every session has written notes, lecture videos, problem-solving videos, practice problems and problem sets with solutions; a full set of exams (including practice exams) with solutions.
- Emphasis on modeling and on interpreting solutions, which is what population, kinetic and gene-circuit models need.

## Coverage

| Unit | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| I: First Order Differential Equations | Modeling physical systems; direction fields; Euler's method; first-order constant-coefficient linear ODEs | [[Ordinary Differential Equation]], [[Direction Field]], [[Euler Method]] |
| II: Second Order Constant Coefficient Linear Equations | Modes and the characteristic equation; interpreting solutions of physical systems | [[Linear Differential Equation]], [[Harmonic Oscillator]] |
| III: Fourier Series and Laplace Transform | Periodic signals and transform methods | [[Differential Equations\|Fourier Series]], [[Differential Equations\|Laplace Transform]] |
| IV: First Order Systems | Autonomous 2x2 linear systems; phase portraits | [[System of Differential Equations]], [[Phase Portrait]], [[Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors]] |

## How to use it

- **Level L2**, after [[MIT 18.01SC - Single Variable Calculus]]; take Unit IV after the eigenvalue part of [[MIT 18.06SC - Linear Algebra]].
- For biology, Units I and IV matter most (growth, kinetics, gene circuits as in [[MIT 8.591J - Systems Biology]]). Unit III is optional on this path.

## Caveats

- The Mathlets are described as Java demonstrations; some may not run in current browsers.
- Session-level titles beyond those quoted were not checked.
