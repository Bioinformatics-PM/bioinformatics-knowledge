# MIT 15.136J - Principles and Practice of Drug Development

> [!abstract]
> MIT graduate course (Fall 2013) describing and critically assessing the stages of developing a pharmaceutical or biopharmaceutical, from discovery to manufacturing, regulation and economics.

## Why this source

- One of the few free university courses that maps the whole drug-development value chain, taught by faculty from clinical, life and management sciences, with industry guests.
- Useful to understand where computational biology fits in industry (discovery, translational and clinical stages).

## How to use it

- **Level M1** (graduate), or end of L3 when preparing an industry internship.
- Read the syllabus and calendar as a map of the field; use the topics to structure notes in the Industry domain.
- Format to imitate in self-study: weekly 3-hour sessions, a team final project (outline, progress report, report, presentation) and a "News of the Week" presentation.

## Caveats

- Instructor names and the exact resource types (slides, readings) were not verified in this session, hence the empty `authors` and tier B.
- Seminar-style graduate course with limited self-study material; regulatory and economic figures from 2013 are dated.
