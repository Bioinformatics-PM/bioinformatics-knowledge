# Coursera Stanford - Algorithms Specialization

> [!abstract]
> Tim Roughgarden's four-course Coursera specialization, based on Stanford's undergraduate algorithms course CS161, covering the fundamentals of algorithm design and analysis.

## Why this source

- The freely auditable online version of Stanford's CS161, with an accompanying book series (*Algorithms Illuminated*).
- Four 4-week courses, a lighter and more guided alternative to MIT's 6.006 plus 6.046J; course 3 treats sequence alignment explicitly as a dynamic programming example.

## How to use it

- **Level L2** (courses 1 and 2) to **L3** (courses 3 and 4), after [[MIT 6.100L - Introduction to CS and Programming Using Python]] and [[MIT 6.042J - Mathematics for Computer Science]].
- Choose one main track: this specialization or [[MIT 6.006 - Introduction to Algorithms]] then [[MIT 6.046J - Design and Analysis of Algorithms]]. Use the other for extra problems.
- Bloom filters and hash tables (course 2) reappear in k-mer indexing; course 3's alignment lectures lead to [[Coursera UCSD - Bioinformatics Specialization]].

## Caveats

- Auditing is free; graded assignments and certificates follow Coursera's paid model.
- `year` is the version currently offered; the launch year was not verified.
