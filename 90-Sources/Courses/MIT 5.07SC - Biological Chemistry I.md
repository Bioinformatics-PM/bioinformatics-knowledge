# MIT 5.07SC - Biological Chemistry I

> [!abstract]
> MIT's OCW Scholar biochemistry course (Fall 2013): protein structure, catalysis and cofactors, then the core metabolic pathways and their regulation.

## Why this source

- Chemistry-first biochemistry: mechanisms and cofactors, not just pathway names. Useful before modeling metabolism or reading pathway databases.
- Organized in modules of sessions; each session has textbook readings, lecture notes or storyboards, a video by JoAnne Stubbe or John Essigmann, and problem sets with solutions.

## Coverage

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Building blocks | Chemical and physical properties of the cell and its building blocks; protein structure | [[Amino Acid]], [[Protein Structure]], [[Protein]] |
| Catalysis | Principles of catalysis; organic and inorganic cofactors | [[Enzyme]], [[Michaelis-Menten Kinetics\|Enzyme Kinetics]], [[Catalysis]], [[Cofactor]] |
| Carbohydrate metabolism | Glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, pentose phosphate pathway | [[Glycolysis]], [[Gluconeogenesis]], [[Pentose Phosphate Pathway]] |
| Lipid metabolism | Fatty acid synthesis and degradation | [[Fatty Acid Metabolism]] |
| Energy metabolism | Krebs cycle, oxidative phosphorylation | [[Citric Acid Cycle]], [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]], [[ATP]] |
| Regulation | Metabolism and regulation in pathways | [[Metabolism]], [[Metabolic Pathway]] |

## How to use it

- **Level L2**, after [[MIT 5.111SC - Principles of Chemical Science]] and [[MIT 7.01SC - Fundamentals of Biology]]; [[MIT 5.12 - Organic Chemistry I]] helps for mechanisms.
- Protein structure and catalysis modules first (they feed structural bioinformatics), then metabolism.

## Caveats

- Which textbook the readings refer to, and whether exams are posted, were not verified.
- Alternative: MIT 7.05 General Biochemistry (Spring 2020) on OCW, whose OCW site holds mainly the metabolism half; its first half is on MITx as 7.05x.
