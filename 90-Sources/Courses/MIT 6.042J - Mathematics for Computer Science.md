# MIT 6.042J - Mathematics for Computer Science

> [!abstract]
> MIT's discrete mathematics course (Spring 2015): proofs, discrete structures and discrete probability, with 130 short tutorial videos and an open textbook.

## Why this source

- The standard entry to mathematical proof and discrete math for computing, with Albert Meyer's 130 short tutorial videos as the main resource.
- Open textbook (*Mathematics for Computer Science*), lecture notes, problem sets and exams.

## Coverage

The course divides roughly into thirds.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental concepts | Definitions, proofs, sets, functions, relations | [[Mathematical Proof]], [[Set]], [[Function]], [[Binary Relation\|Relation]] |
| Discrete structures | Graphs, state machines, modular arithmetic, counting | [[Graph]], [[State Machine]], [[Modular Arithmetic]], [[Combinatorics]] |
| Discrete probability | Probability theory on finite spaces | [[Probability]], [[Conditional Probability]], [[Random Variable]] |

## How to use it

- **Level L1 to L2**, before [[MIT 6.006 - Introduction to Algorithms]].
- Priority: proofs (to read algorithm correctness arguments), graphs and counting. The probability third overlaps with [[MIT 18.05 - Introduction to Probability and Statistics]]; do one of them first.
- Keep the open textbook as a reference.

## Caveats

- Some slide files are labeled for the 2016 run; materials mix runs.
- Availability of exam solutions was not verified.
