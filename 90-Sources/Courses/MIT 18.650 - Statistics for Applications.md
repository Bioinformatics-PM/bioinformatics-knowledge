# MIT 18.650 - Statistics for Applications

> [!abstract]
> Philippe Rigollet's Fall 2016 course giving an in-depth theoretical foundation for statistical methods used in applications, with lecture videos and notes.

## Why this source

- Explains why standard methods work (the role of mathematics in developing efficient statistical methods), which is the step after cookbook statistics.
- Materials: lecture videos, lecture notes (chaptered) and problem sets.

## Coverage

Lecture 1 (Introduction to Statistics) is verified; see Caveats for the rest.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Lecture 1: Introduction to Statistics | [[Probability and Statistics\|Statistics]] |
| Estimation | Parametric inference, maximum likelihood, method of moments | [[Estimator]], [[Maximum Likelihood Estimation]], [[Method of Moments]] |
| Testing | Parametric hypothesis testing, goodness of fit | [[Hypothesis Testing]], [[Chi-Square Test\|Goodness of Fit]] |
| Regression | Linear regression, generalized linear models | [[Linear Regression]], [[Generalized Linear Model]] |
| Bayesian statistics | Priors and posteriors | [[Bayesian Inference]] |

## How to use it

- **Level L3**, after [[MIT 18.05 - Introduction to Probability and Statistics]] or [[MIT 6.041SC - Probabilistic Systems Analysis and Applied Probability]], and [[MIT 18.06SC - Linear Algebra]].
- The regression and GLM part is the theory behind differential expression models; the PCA part links to [[Singular Value Decomposition]].
