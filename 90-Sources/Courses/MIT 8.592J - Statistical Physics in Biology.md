# MIT 8.592J - Statistical Physics in Biology

> [!abstract]
> MIT graduate survey (Spring 2011) of problems at the interface of statistical physics and modern biology, from information in DNA sequences to biopolymer structure, molecular motors and networks.

## Why this source

- Rare course that treats sequence analysis, biopolymer physics and network behavior with one statistical-physics toolkit.
- Materials: lecture notes, problem sets (homework plays an important role), projects and instructor insights.

## Coverage

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Information in DNA | Bioinformatic methods for extracting the information content of DNA; gene finding, sequence comparison, phylogenetic trees | [[Gene Finding]], [[Sequence Alignment]], [[Phylogenetic Tree]] |
| Biopolymers | Physical interactions behind biopolymer structure; DNA double helix, RNA secondary structure, protein folding | [[DNA]], [[RNA Secondary Structure]], [[Protein Folding]] |
| Force, motion, packaging | Protein motors, membranes | [[Molecular Motor]], [[Lipid Bilayer\|Biological Membrane]] |
| Collective behavior | Cellular networks, neural networks, evolution | [[Gene Regulatory Network]], [[Evolution]], [[Statistical Physics\|Statistical Mechanics]] |

## How to use it

- **Level M1**, or late L3 for a strong student, after [[MIT 6.041SC - Probabilistic Systems Analysis and Applied Probability]], a statistical mechanics course, and ideally [[MIT 6.047 - Computational Biology]].
- Read lecture notes in order and attempt every problem set; use the DNA-information and biopolymer parts first.

## Caveats

- Graduate course aimed primarily at physics graduate students: assumes statistical mechanics.
- **No lecture videos**; problem-set solutions not confirmed.
