# MIT 6.046J - Design and Analysis of Algorithms

> [!abstract]
> MIT's intermediate algorithms course (Spring 2015): techniques for designing and analyzing efficient algorithms, from divide-and-conquer and randomization to complexity and cryptography.

## Why this source

- The natural sequel to 6.006, emphasizing methods of application.
- Complete materials: lecture notes, lecture videos, problem sets with solutions, exams with solutions, problem-solving (recitation) videos and instructor insights.

## Coverage

Topics from the course description; lecture titles were not checked individually.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Divide and conquer | Recursive decomposition and its analysis | [[Divide and Conquer]] |
| Randomization | Randomized algorithms and data structures | [[Randomized Algorithm]] |
| Dynamic programming | Advanced dynamic programming | [[Dynamic Programming]] |
| Greedy algorithms | Greedy choice and its proofs | [[Greedy Algorithm]], [[Minimum Spanning Tree]] |
| Incremental improvement | Iterative improvement methods (network flow) | [[Network Flow\|Maximum Flow]] |
| Complexity | Hardness and intractability | [[NP-Completeness]], [[Approximation Algorithm]] |
| Cryptography | Algorithmic cryptography | Cryptography |

## How to use it

- **Level L3**, after [[MIT 6.006 - Introduction to Algorithms]] (stated prerequisite).
- Selective path for bioinformatics: dynamic programming, randomization (hashing, sampling) and complexity (many alignment and phylogeny problems are NP-hard). Cryptography is optional.

## Caveats

- The mapping of "incremental improvement" to network flow and the greedy and complexity sub-links reflect the standard content of these topics, not a checked lecture list.
