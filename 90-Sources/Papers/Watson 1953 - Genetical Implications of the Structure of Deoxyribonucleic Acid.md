# Watson 1953 - Genetical Implications of the Structure of Deoxyribonucleic Acid

> [!abstract]
> Watson and Crick's second 1953 letter: what the double helix implies for genetics, including a copying mechanism and a chemical hypothesis for spontaneous mutation through rare tautomeric forms of the bases.

## Why this source

The first letter ([[Watson 1953 - Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids]]) gave the structure; this one, published five weeks later, drew the genetic consequences. It is the origin of the "rare tautomer" hypothesis: a base that occasionally takes one of its less likely tautomeric forms at the moment the complementary chain is made would pair with the wrong partner, giving a mutation. Direct observation of such tautomeric mispairs in DNA came much later ([[Kimsey 2015 - Visualizing Transient Watson-Crick-Like Mispairs in DNA and RNA Duplexes]]).
