# Hardy 1908 - Mendelian Proportions in a Mixed Population

> [!abstract]
> A one-page letter in which the mathematician G. H. Hardy showed that Mendelian inheritance with random mating keeps genotype proportions stable: the first statement in English of what became the Hardy-Weinberg principle.

## Why this source

The original statement of the principle, short enough to read in a few minutes and to check with simple algebra. Hardy wrote it in reply to an argument by Udny Yule that a dominant character should become more frequent in a population simply because it is dominant, and showed that nothing of the kind follows from Mendelian inheritance alone. Reading it next to a modern derivation shows how little the principle has changed.

## Coverage

Citation: Hardy GH. "Mendelian proportions in a mixed population". *Science* 28(706):49-50 (1908). doi:10.1126/science.28.706.49. A letter to the editor.

## How to use it

- L2: read it after deriving the proportions in [[Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium]]; translate Hardy's $p$, $2q$, $r$ into the genotype frequencies $P$, $H$, $Q$ and check his stability condition, $(H/2)^2 = PQ$.

## Caveats

- Hardy's letters $p$, $q$, $r$ are genotype proportions, not the allele frequencies that modern texts call $p$ and $q$.
- Wilhelm Weinberg derived the same principle independently in 1908, in German; his paper is not in the vault.

The stability condition $q^2 = pr$ is the statement of the letter as widely reproduced; it was not read on the original page. Open-access status not verified.
