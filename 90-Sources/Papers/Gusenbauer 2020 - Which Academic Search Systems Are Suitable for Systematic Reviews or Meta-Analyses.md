# Gusenbauer 2020 - Which Academic Search Systems Are Suitable for Systematic Reviews or Meta-Analyses

> [!abstract]
> An evaluation of 28 widely used academic search systems, including Google Scholar, PubMed and Web of Science, on how precisely, completely and reproducibly they run Boolean searches.

## Why this source

It replaces the folklore about search engines with a systematic comparison, and it explains why the choice of search system matters for [[Systematic Review|systematic reviews]] and [[Meta-Analysis|meta-analyses]].

## Coverage

Citation: Gusenbauer M, Haddaway NR. "Which academic search systems are suitable for systematic reviews or meta-analyses? Evaluating retrieval qualities of Google Scholar, PubMed, and 26 other resources". *Research Synthesis Methods* (2020; online 2019). doi:10.1002/jrsm.1378.

## How to use it

- **L2**: before choosing where to search, read the abstract and the summary table of systems.
- **L3**: when planning a systematic review, pick principal and supplementary search systems with its criteria.

## Caveats

- Search systems change their features; the evaluation describes them as of 2019.
- Verified in this pass: authors, title, journal, DOI, scope (28 systems), criteria and main conclusions. Volume and pages not checked.
