# OECD 1995 - Test No. 107, Partition Coefficient (n-Octanol-Water), Shake Flask Method

> [!abstract]
> The international test guideline for measuring the n-octanol/water partition coefficient of a chemical by shaking it between the two solvents and analysing both phases.

## Why this source

The reference experimental procedure behind the log P values that drug-discovery filters and chemical databases report. It states, in a few pages, what a valid measurement requires: the range where the method works, temperature control, replicate design with different volume ratios, phase separation, analysis, and an acceptance criterion. Reading it shows a learner what is hidden behind a single number in a compound table.

## Coverage

Citation: OECD (1995), *Test No. 107: Partition Coefficient (n-octanol/water): Shake Flask Method*, OECD Guidelines for the Testing of Chemicals, Section 1, OECD Publishing, Paris (1995 edition, as listed in the OECD publication record).

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | $P_{ow}$ for $\log P_{ow}$ between −2 and 4 (occasionally up to 5); unsuitable for surface-active substances | [[Partition Coefficient]] |
| Principle | Nernst partition law, constant at constant temperature, pressure and pH for dilute solutions; dissociation or association of the solute causes deviations | [[Partition Coefficient]], [[Chemical Potential]] |
| Procedure | Temperature between 20 and 25 °C held within ±1 °C; three runs with different n-octanol/water volume ratios, duplicate vessels; phases separated, usually by centrifugation; analysis by photometry, gas chromatography or HPLC | [[Partition Coefficient]] |
| Acceptance | The six $\log P_{ow}$ values must fall within ±0.3 units | [[Partition Coefficient]] |

## How to use it

- **L2**: read the principle and procedure, then redo the mass-balance calculation of a shake-flask experiment ([[Partition Coefficient#Worked example]]).
- **L3**: compare with the guidelines for the HPLC method and for very hydrophobic substances before trusting a log P above the range of this method.

## Caveats

- Verified in this pass: the title, series, adoption year, the log $P_{ow}$ range, the unsuitability for surfactants, the temperature condition, the replicate design, phase separation and analysis methods, the ±0.3 acceptance criterion and the basis in the Nernst partition law, through the OECD publication record and descriptions of the guideline text.
- Access terms (free reading or download) were not checked in this pass. Other OECD guidelines cover log $P_{ow}$ by HPLC and by slow stirring; their ranges were not verified here.
