# Barbas 2007 - Quantitation of DNA and RNA

> [!abstract]
> A short Cold Spring Harbor Protocols information panel giving the absorbance conversion factors and purity ratios used to quantify nucleic acids by UV spectrophotometry.

## Why this source

Textbooks explain why nucleic acids absorb at 260 nm; this panel gives the working numbers a lab uses every day, from a peer-reviewed protocol journal: the concentration that gives an absorbance of 1.0 and the A260/A280 ratios of pure preparations.

## Coverage

Citation: Barbas CF III, Burton DR, Scott JK, Silverman GJ. "Quantitation of DNA and RNA". *Cold Spring Harbor Protocols* (2007), doi:10.1101/pdb.ip47. Adapted from "General Procedures", Appendix 3, in *Phage Display: A Laboratory Manual*.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Conversion factors | For a 1 cm path, OD260 = 1.0 corresponds to 50 µg/mL double-stranded DNA and 40 µg/mL RNA | [[Beer-Lambert Law]] |
| Purity | Pure DNA has OD260/OD280 of about 1.8; pure RNA about 2.0 | [[Beer-Lambert Law]] |

## How to use it

- L1: use the factors to convert an A260 reading into a mass concentration, and the ratio as a first purity check ([[Beer-Lambert Law]]).

## Caveats

- Verified in this pass: authors, title, journal, year, DOI, origin of the text, and the two conversion factors and two purity ratios above. Other content of the panel (for example a factor for single-stranded DNA or oligonucleotides) was not verified and is not cited.
- The factors convert absorbance to mass for long nucleic acids; for a specific molecule, such as a short oligonucleotide, use its own extinction coefficient with the [[Beer-Lambert Law]].
