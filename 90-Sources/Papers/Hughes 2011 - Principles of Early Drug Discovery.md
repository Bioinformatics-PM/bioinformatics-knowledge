# Hughes 2011 - Principles of Early Drug Discovery

> [!abstract]
> A widely cited review of the preclinical stages of drug discovery, from target identification to the selection of a clinical candidate.

## Why this source

A short, clear map of the early pipeline written by industry scientists. It frames drug development as a process that can take 12 to 15 years and cost more than 1 billion US dollars, then walks through each preclinical stage in order. It is the best first read before a textbook such as [[Basic Principles of Drug Discovery and Development (Blass)]].

## How to use it

- L3: read it in one sitting before starting the [[Drug Discovery]] learning path; draw the pipeline as a flowchart and place items 1 to 13 of the MOC on it.
- Come back to the screening and hit-to-lead sections when studying assay quality and structure-activity relationships.

## Caveats

- Early discovery only: clinical trials, approval and post-marketing are outside its scope; use [[MIT 15.136J - Principles and Practice of Drug Development]] for those.
- Written in 2010 to 2011: modalities such as antibodies, oligonucleotides and cell therapies, and AI-driven design, have grown since.
