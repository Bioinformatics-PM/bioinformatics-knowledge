# Goffeau 1996 - Life with 6000 Genes

> [!abstract]
> The report of the complete genome sequence of the budding yeast *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, the first eukaryotic genome to be fully sequenced.

## Why this source

The first complete eukaryotic genome, produced by a worldwide collaboration. Its numbers are the reference scale for a small eukaryote: 12,068 kilobases of sequence on 16 chromosomes, defining 5,885 potential protein-encoding genes, about 140 ribosomal RNA genes, 40 small nuclear RNA genes and 275 transfer RNA genes. Placed between *E. coli* and human, it shows how genome size and gene number scale across the tree of life.

## How to use it

- L1: use the genome size and gene count as the reference small-eukaryote scale in [[Genome]].
- L2: compare the 1996 gene count with the current annotation of the reference strain in a genome database, and explain the difference ([[Gene#Deeper (L2)]]).

## Caveats

- The annotation has been revised since 1996 (genes added and removed); use current database records for gene counts.
- Page numbers vary between bibliographic records; the DOI is the reliable identifier.
- Verified in this pass: authors, journal, volume and issue, DOI and the abstract figures (sequence length, gene counts, 16 chromosomes).
