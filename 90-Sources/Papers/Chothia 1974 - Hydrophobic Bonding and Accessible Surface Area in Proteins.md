# Chothia 1974 - Hydrophobic Bonding and Accessible Surface Area in Proteins

> [!abstract]
> A two-page paper that tied the free energy of the hydrophobic effect in proteins to a geometric quantity, the surface area of nonpolar groups that folding removes from contact with water.

## Why this source

Its starting point is Kauzmann's "hydrophobic bond": the gain in free energy when nonpolar residues move from an aqueous environment into the interior of a protein. By relating this free energy to the accessible surface area that is buried, it turned a thermodynamic idea into something that can be computed from atomic coordinates, which is the logic behind every surface-area estimate of hydrophobic stabilization in structural bioinformatics.

## How to use it

- **L2**: read it after the L1 part of [[Hydrophobic Effect]], as the bridge from thermodynamics to geometry.
- **L3**: compute accessible areas on a real structure ([[Solvent Accessible Surface Area]]) and estimate the hydrophobic contribution of burying them.

## Caveats

- The numerical constant linking free energy to area was not verified in this pass, and later studies proposed different constants: quote a value only with its own source.
- Verified in this pass: the citation (author, journal, volume, pages, date) and the abstract's definition of the hydrophobic bond.
