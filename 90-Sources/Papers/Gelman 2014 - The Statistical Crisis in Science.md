# Gelman 2014 - The Statistical Crisis in Science

> [!abstract]
> A short magazine article by two statisticians arguing that data-dependent analysis choices create a hidden multiple-comparisons problem, the "garden of forking paths", even when no one fishes for significance.

## Why this source

P-hacking is usually described as deliberately trying analyses until one is significant. Gelman and Loken make the subtler point: a researcher may run a single analysis with a hypothesis stated in advance, yet if the details of that analysis would have been chosen differently had the data been different, the reported p-value no longer means what it claims. The article is the accessible version of their 2013 working paper on the garden of forking paths.
