# Hug 2016 - A New View of the Tree of Life

> [!abstract]
> A tree of life of 3,083 genomes from all three domains, built from 16 concatenated ribosomal proteins, in which about a third of the genomes come from uncultivated organisms reconstructed by metagenomics.

## Why this source

It shows what the [[Tree of Life]] looks like once genomes recovered directly from environmental DNA are included: much of the branching diversity is bacterial and comes from lineages never grown in culture, notably a large group called the candidate phyla radiation. It is also a clear example of the genome-scale method: concatenate universally present, mostly vertically inherited genes, align and build one tree ([[Phylogenomics]], [[Metagenomic Binning]]).

## Caveats

- Placement of deep branches (the root, the position of eukaryotes) depends on the genes and models used; the paper is a snapshot of a moving field.
- Author list abbreviated here (first and senior authors); the full list is on the publisher page.
- Metadata, genome counts and the 16-protein method verified in this pass; open-access status from the publisher page as remembered, not re-checked.
