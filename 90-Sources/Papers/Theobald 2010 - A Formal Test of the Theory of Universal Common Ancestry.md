# Theobald 2010 - A Formal Test of the Theory of Universal Common Ancestry

> [!abstract]
> A statistical test of universal common ancestry: model selection on the sequences of universally conserved proteins favours a single common ancestor of the three domains over models of independent origins.

## Why this source

It turns [[Common Descent]] at its largest scale into a quantitative hypothesis test that does not assume a tree in advance: models in which all life shares one ancestor are compared with models in which the domains have separate origins, using sequence data and model-selection criteria. A worked example of how sequence statistics address a historical question ([[Model Selection]], [[Sequence Homology]]).

## How to use it

L3: read after [[Common Descent]] and [[Sequence Homology]], together with a critique of the method, as a case study in turning an evolutionary claim into a model comparison.

## Caveats

- Later work argued that the test's reliance on aligned sequences biases it toward common ancestry, because alignment itself creates similarity; treat the result as strong support from one framework, not as a proof.
