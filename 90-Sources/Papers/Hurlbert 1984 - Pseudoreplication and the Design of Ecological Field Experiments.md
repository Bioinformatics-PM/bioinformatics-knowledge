# Hurlbert 1984 - Pseudoreplication and the Design of Ecological Field Experiments

> [!abstract]
> The paper that named pseudoreplication: using inferential statistics on data where treatments are not replicated or replicates are not independent.

## Why this source

Although written for field ecology, it describes the most common design error in laboratory biology and omics: counting measurements as if they were independent experimental units. Hurlbert surveyed 176 experimental studies and found pseudoreplication in 27% of them, 48% of those that applied inferential statistics. The vocabulary it introduced is now standard in experimental design teaching.

## How to use it

- L2: read the definitions and the classification of pseudoreplication types, then translate one example to a lab setting (three wells from one culture are not three biological replicates).
- Read it with [[Experimental Design for Laboratory Biologists (Lazic)]], which applies the same ideas to lab experiments.

## Caveats

- Examples are ecological (plots, tanks, animals); the translation to cells, mice or sequencing libraries is left to the reader.
- Long (25 pages); the first sections carry the essential ideas.
