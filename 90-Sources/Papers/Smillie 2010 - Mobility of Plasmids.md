# Smillie 2010 - Mobility of Plasmids

> [!abstract]
> A computational survey of how sequenced plasmids move between cells: which carry a complete conjugation system, which can only be mobilized by others, and which cannot transfer at all.

## Why this source

It turns the textbook distinction between conjugative and mobilizable plasmids into a classification that can be applied to sequences, based on the genetic modules for conjugation (relaxase and mating-pair formation genes), and it estimates how common each class is among sequenced plasmids.

## How to use it

- L2: read the abstract and introduction for the conjugative, mobilizable and nonmobilizable classes.
- L3: read the classification of relaxases and mating-pair formation systems before annotating the transfer genes of a plasmid sequence.

## Caveats

- The proportions depend on the plasmid sequences available in 2010, which over-represent proteobacteria and clinically relevant species.
- Verified in this pass (web search): authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, DOI, PMC record, the size of the dataset, the four-by-six classification and the proportions above. The six relaxase family names are given in a companion review and were not checked against this paper.
