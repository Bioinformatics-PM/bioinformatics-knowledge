# Relling 2011 - Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium

> [!abstract]
> The paper that introduced CPIC, the consortium that writes peer-reviewed, evidence-based and freely available guidelines for adjusting drugs to a patient's genotype.

## Why this source

Pharmacogenetic tests were adopted slowly in clinical practice, partly because there were no specific guidelines on how to change a prescription once a genotype is known. This paper explains why CPIC was created within the Pharmacogenomics Research Network and how its guidelines for gene and drug pairs are meant to work: peer-reviewed, updated, evidence-based and freely accessible. It is the entry point to the whole CPIC guideline series.

## How to use it

- L3: read it for the rationale, then open one current CPIC guideline (for example a CYP2C19 or CYP2D6 guideline, published as separate papers) and follow the path from genotype to star alleles, metabolizer phenotype and dose recommendation.
- Current guidelines and gene tables are on the CPIC website (cpicpgx.org).

## Caveats

- Describes the consortium, not the content of any single guideline; cite the specific guideline for a drug recommendation.
- Guidelines are updated regularly; always check the current version.
