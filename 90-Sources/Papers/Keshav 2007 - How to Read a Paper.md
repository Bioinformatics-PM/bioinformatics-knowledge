# Keshav 2007 - How to Read a Paper

> [!abstract]
> A short guide proposing to read a research paper in up to three passes of increasing depth instead of from beginning to end, and applying the method to a literature survey.

## Why this source

Short, concrete and widely assigned in graduate courses, it gives a method a beginner can apply the same day. It comes from computer science, so [[Reading a Scientific Paper]] adapts it to figure-centred biology papers.

## Coverage

Citation: Keshav S. "How to read a paper". *ACM SIGCOMM Computer Communication Review* 37(3) (July 2007). doi:10.1145/1273445.1273458.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| First pass | A quick scan of about five to ten minutes for a bird's-eye view: title, abstract, introduction, headings, conclusions, references | [[Reading a Scientific Paper]] |
| Second pass | Up to an hour: grasp the content, look carefully at the figures, but not the details | [[Reading a Scientific Paper]] |
| Third pass | In-depth understanding, by virtually re-implementing the work and challenging its assumptions | [[Reading a Scientific Paper]], [[Critical Appraisal]] |
| Literature survey | Using the passes and a search engine to survey a new field, starting from a few recent papers and their shared citations | [[Literature Search]] |

## How to use it

- **L1**: read it once (it is a few pages long), then apply the first pass to every paper you open.
- **L2**: use the literature-survey section when entering a new field.

## Caveats

- Written for computer-science papers: biology papers carry more of their evidence in figures and methods.
- An editorial note, not a research article: it states a method, not evidence that the method works best.
- Verified in this pass: citation, DOI, the three passes with their durations and goals, and the literature-survey use. The detailed first-pass checklist and page numbers were not re-checked line by line.
- Copies are hosted on many university course pages; open access on the publisher site not checked.
