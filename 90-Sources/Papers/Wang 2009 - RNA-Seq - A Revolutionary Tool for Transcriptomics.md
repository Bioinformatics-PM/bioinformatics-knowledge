# Wang 2009 - RNA-Seq - A Revolutionary Tool for Transcriptomics

> [!abstract]
> The review that introduced RNA-Seq to a broad audience: profiling the transcriptome by deep sequencing, and why it measures transcripts and their isoforms more precisely than earlier methods.

## Why this source

Written when RNA-Seq was new, it states what the transcriptome is and what sequencing changed in measuring it. It is the short, citable entry point to the transcriptome layer of [[Omics]] before the full [[Transcriptomics]] syllabus.

## Coverage

Citation: Wang Z, Gerstein M, Snyder M. "RNA-Seq: a revolutionary tool for transcriptomics". *Nature Reviews Genetics* 10(1):57-63 (January 2009). Free at PMC2949280.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| The transcriptome | The complete set of transcripts in a cell, and their quantities, for a given developmental stage or physiological condition | [[Transcriptome]], [[Omics]] |
| RNA-Seq | Transcriptome profiling with deep-sequencing technologies; more precise measurement of transcript and isoform levels than other methods | [[RNA Sequencing]], [[Alternative Splicing]] |

## How to use it

- **L1**: read the introduction for the definition of the transcriptome.
- **L2**: read it before [[RNA Sequencing]] to see which questions the method was designed to answer.

## Caveats

- Written at the very start of RNA-Seq: platforms, read lengths and costs are dated, and single-cell and long-read transcriptomics are absent.
- Verified in this pass: authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages, year, PMC record and abstract.
