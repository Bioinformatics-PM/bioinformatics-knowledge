# Elliott 2015 - The C-Value Enigma and the Evolution of Eukaryotic Genome Content

> [!abstract]
> A review of why eukaryotic genome sizes vary more than 60,000-fold, how the "C-value paradox" was resolved by the discovery of non-coding DNA, and which questions remain.

## Why this source

The clearest modern account of genome-size variation by researchers of the field. Its argument: eukaryotic genomes differ from bacterial and archaeal ones in chromosome structure, repetitive DNA and introns, and above all in size; the enormous variation in size is not explained by gene number. Large differences between morphologically similar species were noticed in the late 1940s and early 1950s and called the "C-value paradox" about twenty years later. The discovery of vast amounts of non-protein-coding DNA resolved the paradox but opened the questions now called the C-value enigma: which sequences make up genomes, which contribute to size variation, and how they accumulate or are lost.
