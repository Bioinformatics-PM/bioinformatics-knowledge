# Hanahan 2011 - Hallmarks of Cancer - The Next Generation

> [!abstract]
> The review that organizes the biology of cancer into a small set of acquired capabilities, the hallmarks, together with the enabling characteristics that let tumors acquire them.

## Why this source

The hallmarks are the shared vocabulary of cancer biology and cancer genomics: driver mutations, pathways and therapies are routinely sorted by the capability they affect. This 2011 update of the 2000 paper is the version most often cited, and its abstract alone gives a complete map for a first course.

## How to use it

- **L2**: read the abstract and use the six hallmarks as a checklist when a note explains why a cellular process matters in cancer ([[Apoptosis]], [[Cell Cycle]]).
- **L3**: map the drivers of a cancer genomics study onto the hallmarks ([[Oncogene]], [[Tumor Suppressor Gene]]).

## Caveats

- A conceptual framework, not a list of mechanisms: each hallmark covers many molecular routes, and the list has been extended since 2011.
- Verified in this pass (web search): the citation, DOI and PMID, and the content of the abstract (the six hallmarks, the two enabling characteristics, the two emerging hallmarks, the tumor microenvironment). The body of the review was not read.
