# Beadle 1941 - Genetic Control of Biochemical Reactions in Neurospora

> [!abstract]
> The *Neurospora* mutant experiments showing that genes control individual biochemical steps, the origin of the "one gene, one enzyme" idea.

## Why this source

Beadle and Tatum induced mutations in the bread mold *Neurospora* and isolated strains that could no longer make an essential nutrient. Each mutation blocked a single step of a biosynthetic pathway and was inherited as a single gene. This turned the abstract Mendelian factor into something with a chemical job, and founded biochemical genetics. Beadle and Tatum shared the 1958 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Joshua Lederberg.

## How to use it

- L1: read the short paper for the history of the [[Gene]] concept; draw a pathway and mark where each mutant is blocked.
- L2: explain why "one gene, one enzyme" became "one gene, one polypeptide" and then broke down with RNA genes and alternative splicing, using [[Gerstein 2007 - What Is a Gene, Post-ENCODE]].

## Caveats

- The slogan "one gene, one enzyme" oversimplifies: many proteins have several subunits, and many genes make RNA or several products.
