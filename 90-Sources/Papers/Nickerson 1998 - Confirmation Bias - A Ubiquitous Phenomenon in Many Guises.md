# Nickerson 1998 - Confirmation Bias - A Ubiquitous Phenomenon in Many Guises

> [!abstract]
> The standard review of confirmation bias: the seeking or interpreting of evidence in ways that are partial to existing beliefs, expectations or a hypothesis in hand, documented across laboratory tasks and practical contexts.

## Why this source

Psychology has studied confirmation bias under many names and tasks. This review gathers them under one definition, describes the forms the bias takes (which evidence people seek, how they weigh and interpret it) and discusses its operation in practical settings, including science. It is the reference that most later work cites for the term.

## How to use it

- L1: read the abstract and introduction for the definition, then the part on hypothesis-testing tasks.
- L2: read the part on science alongside [[Platt 1964 - Strong Inference]].

## Caveats

- A narrative review from 1998: later work refined some interpretations (for example, whether testing positive cases is always a bias), and the replication debate that followed is not covered.
- Paid on the publisher's site; free copies circulate on university course pages.
