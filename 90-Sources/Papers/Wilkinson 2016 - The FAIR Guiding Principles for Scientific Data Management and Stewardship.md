# Wilkinson 2016 - The FAIR Guiding Principles for Scientific Data Management and Stewardship

> [!abstract]
> The first formal publication of the FAIR principles: data and metadata should be Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable, by people and by machines.

## Why this source

A short Comment, jointly endorsed by stakeholders from academia, industry, funding agencies and publishers, that became the reference vocabulary of research data policy. Its distinctive point is machine-actionability: FAIR data can be found and used by software, not only by a human reading a web page. Funders and data management plans now ask for FAIR data by name.

## How to use it

- L2: read the principles box and score one dataset you have downloaded (for example from [[European Nucleotide Archive]]) against each letter.
- L3: pair it with [[Carroll 2020 - The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance]], which adds people and purpose to FAIR's data-centric view.

## Caveats

- FAIR is not "open": data can be FAIR and under controlled access.
- The principles say what to achieve, not how; implementation depends on community standards and repositories.
- Metadata (title, journal, article number, DOI, date) verified in this pass; the journal is open access.
