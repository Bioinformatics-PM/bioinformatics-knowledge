# Kimura 1968 - Evolutionary Rate at the Molecular Level

> [!abstract]
> The founding paper of the neutral theory of molecular evolution: most molecular changes fixed in evolution are selectively neutral and fixed by random drift.

## Why this source

It reframed molecular evolution around mutation and [[Genetic Drift|drift]] rather than selection alone, and it predicts a [[Molecular Clock|molecular clock]]. The neutral theory is the null model behind most tests for selection in genomics.

## Coverage

Citation: Kimura M. *Nature* 217(5129):624-626 (1968). doi:10.1038/217624a0. PubMed 5637732.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Observation | Rates of amino-acid substitution estimated from protein comparisons are high | [[Molecular Evolution]] |
| Argument | High rates are best explained by fixation of neutral mutations by drift | [[Neutral Theory of Molecular Evolution]], [[Genetic Drift]], [[Fixation Probability]] |
| Consequence | Substitution rate equals the neutral mutation rate, independent of population size, giving a clock-like rate | [[Molecular Clock]], [[Mutation Rate]] |

> [!example] The core argument
> In a haploid population of $N$ individuals with neutral mutation rate $\mu$ per individual per generation, $\mu N$ new neutral mutations arise each generation, each fixed with probability $1/N$. The substitution rate is therefore $\mu N \times 1/N = \mu$.

## How to use it

- L2: read with the population-genetics chapters of [[Principles of Population Genetics (Hartl)]].
- L3: use as the null hypothesis when studying dN/dS and other selection tests in [[Molecular Evolution (Yang)]].

## Caveats

- The strict neutral theory was later refined (nearly neutral theory, Ohta); the neutralist-selectionist debate is ongoing.
- The example callout is the standard textbook derivation, not a quotation from the paper.
