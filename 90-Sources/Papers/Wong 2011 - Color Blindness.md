# Wong 2011 - Color Blindness

> [!abstract]
> A one-page *Nature Methods* "Points of View" column giving practical rules and an eight-color palette for figures that readers with red-green color blindness can read.

## Why this source

Short, concrete and written for life scientists making figures. It explains that the two most common inherited forms of color blindness, protanopia and deuteranopia (red-green defects), affect up to 8 % of men and 0.5 % of women of Northern European ancestry, and presents a palette of eight colors that remain distinguishable for them. The palette originates from Okabe and Ito's Color Universal Design work.

## Caveats

- One page: it gives rules of thumb, not a perceptual study.
- Part of the "Points of View" column series in *Nature Methods*; other columns of the series cover other aspects of figure design and are separate sources.
- Verified in this pass: author, title, journal, volume, issue, page, DOI, the prevalence figures and the purpose of the palette. The hexadecimal values used in the vault are the widely reproduced values of this palette.
