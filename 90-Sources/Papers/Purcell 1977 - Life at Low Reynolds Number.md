# Purcell 1977 - Life at Low Reynolds Number

> [!abstract]
> A classic lecture by E. M. Purcell on how microorganisms move through water when inertia is negligible, which states the scallop theorem: a swimmer that can only make a reciprocal motion cannot swim.

## Why this source

The standard reference on the physics of swimming microorganisms. With almost no equations, it explains why a bacterium cannot coast and why a stroke that is simply reversed gets nowhere at low Reynolds number. It is the best entry point to the topic for a biologist, before specialized reviews of microswimmer hydrodynamics.

## Coverage

Citation: Purcell EM. "Life at low Reynolds number." *American Journal of Physics* 45(1):3-11 (1977). doi:10.1119/1.10903.

| Part | Content | Vault notes |
|---|---|---|
| Low Reynolds number | For microorganisms in water inertia is negligible: motion stops when the force stops, and coasting is impossible | [[Reynolds Number]], [[Viscosity]] |
| Scallop theorem | A body with a single hinge (one degree of freedom) can only make a reciprocal motion and cannot swim, whatever the speeds of its opening and closing strokes | [[Reynolds Number]] |
| Non-reciprocal swimming | Swimming needs a sequence of shapes that is not simply reversed; Purcell proposes a three-link swimmer with two hinges | [[Reynolds Number]], [[Bacteria]] |

## How to use it

- L2: read it after [[Viscosity]] and [[Reynolds Number]]; it is the edited transcript of a talk and reads in one sitting.
- L3: use it as the conceptual starting point for the hydrodynamics of swimming cells, flagella and cilia.

## Caveats

- An informal talk, not a research article: arguments are qualitative and numbers are orders of magnitude.
- Published by AIP Publishing; the publisher's version may require a subscription.
- Verified in this pass: bibliographic data, the low-Reynolds-number theme (no coasting), the scallop theorem and the three-link swimmer, from the publisher's record and secondary accounts of the paper. Individual numbers in the paper were not checked.
