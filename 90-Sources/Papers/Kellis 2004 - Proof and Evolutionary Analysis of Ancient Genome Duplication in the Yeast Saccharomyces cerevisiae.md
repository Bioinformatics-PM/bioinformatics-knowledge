# Kellis 2004 - Proof and Evolutionary Analysis of Ancient Genome Duplication in the Yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae

> [!abstract]
> The comparative-genomics paper that proved that the baker's yeast genome descends from a whole-genome duplication, and then used the surviving duplicate pairs to watch paralogs diverge.

## Why this source

By sequencing *Kluyveromyces waltii*, a yeast that diverged before the duplication, the authors found a 1:2 mapping between the genomes: each region of *K. waltii* corresponds to two regions of *S. cerevisiae*, as expected after a whole-genome duplication. The duplicate pairs then became a natural experiment on paralog evolution: in 95% of the cases of accelerated evolution, only one member of a gene pair is affected. The authors take this as strong support for a specific model of duplicate evolution and use it to distinguish ancestral from derived functions. For a learner it is the clearest data-driven picture of how paralogs diverge, and an example of using an outgroup genome to tell which copy changed.
