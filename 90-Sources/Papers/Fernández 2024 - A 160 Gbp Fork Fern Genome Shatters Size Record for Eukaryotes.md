# Fernández 2024 - A 160 Gbp Fork Fern Genome Shatters Size Record for Eukaryotes

> [!abstract]
> The measurement of the largest eukaryotic genome known, 160.45 Gbp per haploid nucleus (1C) in the New Caledonian fork fern *Tmesipteris oblanceolata*.

## Why this source

It fixes the upper end of the eukaryotic genome-size range with a recent, peer-reviewed measurement: 160.45 Gbp/1C, about 7% larger than the previous record holder, the flowering plant *Paris japonica* (148.89 Gbp), and more than 50 times the human genome. It is the natural companion of [[Elliott 2015 - The C-Value Enigma and the Evolution of Eukaryotic Genome Content]] when discussing why genome size is not a measure of complexity.

## How to use it

- L1: use the record as the upper bound of the genome-size table in [[Genome]].
- L2: compare it with the human genome and with gene numbers to state the C-value paradox in numbers.

## Caveats

- A genome size estimated from nuclear DNA content, not a genome sequence: no gene count is available for this species.
- Records change as more species are measured; quote the year with the value.
- Verified in this pass: authors, journal, volume, issue, article number, DOI, the 160.45 Gbp/1C value and the comparison with *Paris japonica*.
