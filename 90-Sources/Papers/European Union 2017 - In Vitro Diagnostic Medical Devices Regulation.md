# European Union 2017 - In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation

> [!abstract]
> The EU regulation governing in vitro diagnostic medical devices, including genetic tests and the software that analyzes their data.

## Why this source

A clinical genomic test, and often the bioinformatics pipeline behind it, is an in vitro diagnostic device in EU law. The IVDR replaced Directive 98/79/EC with a risk-based system: devices are classified in classes A to D, most need assessment by a notified body, and manufacturers must demonstrate performance with evidence. It also sets the conditions under which health institutions may use tests they make in house.

## How to use it

- L3: read the recitals and the definitions first, then the classification rules and the in-house exemption; answer item 6 of [[Regulation and Standards]].
- Read it next to the companion Medical Device Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2017/745 ([[Medical Device Regulation]]).

## Caveats

- Transitional provisions have been amended since 2017; always read the consolidated version on EUR-Lex and the Commission's guidance.
- A legal text of about 150 pages: guidance documents from the Medical Device Coordination Group explain how it applies to software.
