# Wright 1931 - Evolution in Mendelian Populations

> [!abstract]
> Sewall Wright's long 1931 paper, the first detailed quantitative theory of how mutation, migration, selection and population size change gene frequencies, in which genetic drift, effective population size and inbreeding are developed.

## Why this source

One of the founding texts of population genetics. It makes population size an evolutionary force alongside mutation, migration and selection, and it develops the ideas of random drift, effective population size and inbreeding on which every later treatment builds. It is the primary source for the history of [[Genetic Drift]], [[Effective Population Size]], [[Inbreeding]] and [[Gene Flow]].

## How to use it

- L2: read the introduction for the program of the paper; for the derivations, the textbook treatment of [[Principles of Population Genetics (Hartl)]] is easier.
- L3: read the parts on population size and inbreeding after [[Effective Population Size]] and [[Inbreeding]].

## Caveats

- 1931 notation and style: long, mathematically dense, and written before the coalescent and molecular data; modern formulations come from later work and textbooks.
