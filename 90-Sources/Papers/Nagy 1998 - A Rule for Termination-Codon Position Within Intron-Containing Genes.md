# Nagy 1998 - A Rule for Termination-Codon Position Within Intron-Containing Genes

> [!abstract]
> The short paper that stated the "50-55 nucleotide rule" of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay in mammalian cells: a premature stop codon triggers decay only if it lies more than 50-55 nucleotides upstream of the last exon-exon junction.

## Why this source

It turned scattered observations on nonsense mutations into a positional rule that can be computed from a transcript model. By comparing where nonsense codons did or did not reduce mRNA abundance in several mammalian genes (triose phosphate isomerase, β-globin, mouse major urinary protein, glutathione peroxidase 1), Nagy and Maquat concluded that only termination codons located more than 50-55 nucleotides upstream of the 3'-most exon-exon junction mediate mRNA decay. This rule is still the starting point of every in silico prediction of whether a nonsense or frameshift variant leads to decay or to a truncated protein.

## Caveats

- A rule of thumb for mammalian intron-containing genes: later work described exceptions and junction-independent routes to decay, so treat the prediction as a hypothesis.
- The threshold is a range (50-55 nt), not a sharp cut-off.
