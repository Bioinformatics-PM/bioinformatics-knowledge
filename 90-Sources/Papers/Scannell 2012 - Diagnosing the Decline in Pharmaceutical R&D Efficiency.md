# Scannell 2012 - Diagnosing the Decline in Pharmaceutical R&D Efficiency

> [!abstract]
> The analysis that named Eroom's law: the number of new drugs approved per billion US dollars of R&D has halved roughly every nine years since 1950.

## Why this source

It quantified a long-term paradox: despite large advances in the science, technology and management that should raise R&D efficiency, inflation-adjusted efficiency fell about 80-fold. The authors named the trend "Eroom's law" (Moore spelled backwards) and proposed four causes, which makes the paper a structured argument rather than a lament.

## How to use it

- L3: read it alongside [[NHGRI DNA Sequencing Costs]]: one curve fell by orders of magnitude while the other rose, which is the tension item 14 of [[Innovation and Entrepreneurship]] asks you to explain.
- Relate cause 4 to [[Nelson 2015 - The Support of Human Genetic Evidence for Approved Drug Indications]], which argues for better target selection rather than more screening.

## Caveats

- Diagnosis, not a validated causal model: the four causes are argued, not tested.
- Industry data up to about 2010; later trends in approvals and costs are debated.
- Metadata (authors, journal, volume, pages, DOI) verified in this pass; access appears to be subscription-based.
