# Wasserstein 2016 - The ASA Statement on p-Values

> [!abstract]
> "The ASA's Statement on p-Values: Context, Process, and Purpose": the American Statistical Association's official statement of six principles on what p-values mean and do not mean, with the story of how it was written.

## Why this source

The first formal statement by a major statistical society on the use of p-values, written for researchers, practitioners and science writers who are not statisticians. It gives an informal definition of the p-value and six short principles that summarize the most common misreadings, which is exactly what a learner needs before using p-values on thousands of genes.

## How to use it

- L1: read the six principles right after computing your first p-values ([[P-Value]]).
- L2: reread them before interpreting a genome-wide screen, together with [[Ioannidis 2005 - Why Most Published Research Findings Are False]].

## Caveats

- A consensus statement, not a technical paper: it states principles without proofs or worked examples.
- Verified in this pass: authors, title, journal, volume, issue, pages and DOI. Free copies circulate widely; the open-access status of the publisher page was not checked.
