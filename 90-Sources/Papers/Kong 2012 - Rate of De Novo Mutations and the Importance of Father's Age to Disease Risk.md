# Kong 2012 - Rate of De Novo Mutations and the Importance of Father's Age to Disease Risk

> [!abstract]
> Whole-genome sequencing of 78 Icelandic parent-offspring trios, giving the human germline mutation rate and showing that it rises with the father's age.

## Why this source

A direct measurement of human mutation from sequencing families rather than from comparisons between species. With an average paternal age of 29.7 years, the de novo rate was 1.20 × 10⁻⁸ per nucleotide per generation. Variation between children was dominated by the father's age, about two extra mutations per year, with an exponential model doubling paternal mutations every 16.5 years. The authors discussed the consequences for diseases such as schizophrenia and autism.
