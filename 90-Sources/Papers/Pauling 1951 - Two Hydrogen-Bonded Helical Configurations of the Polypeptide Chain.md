# Pauling 1951 - Two Hydrogen-Bonded Helical Configurations of the Polypeptide Chain

> [!abstract]
> The paper that proposed the α helix: from the planar geometry of the peptide unit and the requirement that backbone groups form hydrogen bonds, it derived helical structures of the polypeptide chain before any protein structure had been solved at atomic detail.

## Why this source

The landmark of model building in structural biology: the structure was predicted from stereochemical rules (bond lengths, planar peptide, hydrogen bonds), not fitted to a protein structure. It explains why secondary structures exist at all, which is the starting point of [[Protein Secondary Structure]].

## Coverage

Citation: Pauling L, Corey RB, Branson HR. "The structure of proteins: two hydrogen-bonded helical configurations of the polypeptide chain". *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the USA* 37(4):205-211 (April 1951). doi:10.1073/pnas.37.4.205. PMID 14816373.

## How to use it

- **L2**: read the introduction and the description of the 3.7-residue helix after the Core of [[Protein Secondary Structure]]; compare its value with the 3.6 residues per turn measured later in proteins.
- **L3**: use it as a case study of hypothesis-driven modeling, before [[Ramachandran Plot]].

## Caveats

- A 1951 model: the α helix is described today with 3.6 residues per turn (see [[Biochemistry (Berg)]]); use the paper for the reasoning, textbooks for current values.
- The companion β-sheet ("pleated sheet") paper of Pauling and Corey is a separate 1951 publication, not covered by this note.
