# Fitch 1970 - Distinguishing Homologous from Analogous Proteins

> [!abstract]
> The paper that split homology into orthology (homologs separated by speciation) and paralogy (homologs separated by gene duplication), the distinction on which genome annotation and comparative genomics rest.

## Why this source

It is the origin of the two words every comparative analysis uses. Fitch proposed that homologous genes fall into two classes according to the event that separated them: when the homology results from gene duplication the genes are paralogous, when it results from speciation they are orthologous. His own examples are α- and β-hemoglobin (paralogs) and the α-hemoglobins of human and mouse (orthologs). For a learner, the definitions are short, precise and based on history, not on function, which is exactly what later textbook paraphrases often lose.

## Coverage

Citation: Fitch WM. "Distinguishing homologous from analogous proteins". *Systematic Zoology* 19(2):99-113 (June 1970). doi:10.2307/2412448. The journal is now *Systematic Biology*.
