# Chandler 2005 - Interfaces and the Driving Force of Hydrophobic Assembly

> [!abstract]
> A review of the physics of the hydrophobic effect, showing that it works differently for small nonpolar molecules hydrated one by one and for large nonpolar surfaces that assemble.

## Why this source

Textbooks describe the hydrophobic effect with small solutes and an ordered water shell. This review explains why that picture does not scale: around a small solute, water keeps its hydrogen-bond network, whereas a large nonpolar surface forces water to give up hydrogen bonds and form an interface. It is the reference for the L3 view of hydrophobic assembly of proteins and membranes as a length-scale-dependent phenomenon.
