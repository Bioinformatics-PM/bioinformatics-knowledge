# Crick 1958 - On Protein Synthesis

> [!abstract]
> Crick's 1957 lecture, published in 1958, that stated both the sequence hypothesis and the central dogma of molecular biology.

## Why this source

Delivered at a Society for Experimental Biology symposium in September 1957, before the genetic code was known, it set the logic of molecular biology. The **sequence hypothesis** says that the order of bases in a nucleic acid codes for the amino acid sequence of a protein. The **central dogma** says that once sequence information has passed into protein it cannot get out again. Reading the original shows that these are two distinct claims, which later retellings often merge.

## How to use it

- L1: read the sections stating the two hypotheses; write each in one sentence of your own.
- L2: compare with the 1970 restatement in [[Crick 1970 - Central Dogma of Molecular Biology]], which clarifies the allowed and forbidden transfers after the discovery of reverse transcription.

## Caveats

- Conference proceedings without a DOI; the NLM copy is the practical way to read it.
- Many biochemical details are superseded; read it for the logic, not for mechanisms.
