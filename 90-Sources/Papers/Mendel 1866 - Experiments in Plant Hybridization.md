# Mendel 1866 - Experiments in Plant Hybridization

> [!abstract]
> The founding paper of genetics: crosses between true-breeding varieties of garden pea showing that hereditary "elements" separate in gametes and combine in fixed numerical ratios.

## Why this source

The primary data behind the 3:1 and 9:3:3:1 ratios, reported as raw counts that can still be tested today. Reading the original shows how Mendel reasoned from counts to a model, which is the logic every later genetic analysis follows.

## Coverage

Citation: Mendel G. "Versuche über Pflanzen-Hybriden". *Verhandlungen des naturforschenden Vereines in Brünn*, Bd. IV für das Jahr 1865, Abhandlungen, 3-47 (published 1866). Read at the meetings of the Brünn Natural History Society of 8 February and 8 March 1865. English translation "Experiments in Plant Hybridization" (C. T. Druery and William Bateson, 1901), revised by Roger Blumberg for the MendelWeb project.

## How to use it

- L1: read the sections on the F1 and F2 generations and the combination of several characters, then recompute each ratio.
- L2: test each ratio with a chi-square test (Exercise in [[Mendelian Inheritance]]).

## Caveats

- Mendel wrote of "elements" and "factors"; the words gene and allele came later. Translations differ in wording; MendelWeb documents its corrections to the 1901 text.
- Verified in this pass: the publication record (journal, volume, pages, meeting dates), the history of the English translation and MendelWeb, and the counts listed above (checked against textbook reproductions of Mendel's tables, not on the MendelWeb page itself). Other counts of the paper were not checked.
