# Cleveland 1984 - Graphical Perception

> [!abstract]
> The study that ranked how accurately people decode quantities from graphs depending on the visual encoding (position, length, angle, area and others), founding evidence-based chart design.

## Why this source

It replaces taste with measurement. The authors identified elementary perceptual tasks that a reader performs when extracting numbers from a graph, ordered them by how accurately people perform them, and tested the ordering experimentally: judgments of position along a scale were the most accurate, followed by length, then angle and slope, with area judgments last. They used the ordering to guide the design of graphical methods.

## Caveats

- Experiments of 1984 on simple static graphs; color, interaction and very large data sets were not the subject.
