# Koonin 2005 - Orthologs, Paralogs, and Evolutionary Genomics

> [!abstract]
> A review of orthology and paralogy for the genomic era: definitions and subtypes, the main methods to identify them, and their evolutionary and functional implications.

## Why this source

Orthologs and paralogs are two fundamentally different types of homologous genes, which evolved respectively by vertical descent from a single ancestral gene and by duplication. Koonin argues that a clear distinction between them is critical for a robust evolutionary classification of genes and for reliable functional annotation of newly sequenced genomes, and notes that genome comparisons establish orthologous relationships with genes from taxonomically distant species for the majority of the genes of each sequenced genome. It is the reference for the vocabulary that goes beyond Fitch's two words.
