# Platt 1964 - Strong Inference

> [!abstract]
> An essay arguing that fields advance fastest when they systematically devise alternative hypotheses and crucial experiments that exclude some of them.

## Why this source

Platt noticed that some fields, molecular biology and high-energy physics among them, were moving much faster than others, and attributed the difference to method rather than talent. His "strong inference" is a disciplined form of falsification applied to several hypotheses at once. It gives a vocabulary for judging whether an experiment is decisive or merely consistent with an idea.

## How to use it

- L3: read it for item 17 of [[Scientific Method]]; then reread [[Meselson 1958 - The Replication of DNA in Escherichia coli]] as a textbook case (three models, one experiment that excludes two).
- Before a Lab benchmark, write the competing hypotheses and the result that would exclude each.

## Caveats

- An essay with anecdotes, not an empirical comparison of fields; its claim about why some fields progress faster has been criticized.
- Strong inference fits questions with a small set of well-defined alternatives better than exploratory, data-driven research.
