# Avery 1944 - Chemical Nature of the Substance Inducing Transformation of Pneumococcal Types

> [!abstract]
> The paper that identified the "transforming principle" of pneumococcus as DNA: a purified fraction that changes one bacterial type into another, whose activity resists protein- and RNA-degrading enzymes but is destroyed by DNA-degrading ones.

## Why this source

The first strong chemical evidence that genes are made of DNA, obtained by purification and by a set of enzyme treatments, each of which removes one candidate substance while leaving everything else unchanged. It is a clean example of evidence built by elimination of alternatives, and of a controlled comparison.

## Coverage

Citation: Avery OT, MacLeod CM, McCarty M. "Studies on the Chemical Nature of the Substance Inducing Transformation of Pneumococcal Types: Induction of Transformation by a Desoxyribonucleic Acid Fraction Isolated from Pneumococcus Type III". *Journal of Experimental Medicine* 79(2):137-158 (1 February 1944).

## How to use it

- L1: read the summary and the enzyme results; list which candidate substance each treatment rules out.
- L2: discuss what a skeptic could still object (trace contaminants, crude enzyme preparations) and which experiment would answer each objection.

## Caveats

- The DNA-degrading preparations used here were crude, not purified enzymes; a follow-up paper by McCarty and Avery (1946) examined the effect of purified desoxyribonuclease.
