# Manning 2002 - The Protein Kinase Complement of the Human Genome

> [!abstract]
> The first catalog of the human "kinome": every protein kinase gene found in the human genome sequence, mapped to chromosomes, disease loci and cancer amplicons.

## Why this source

Protein kinases are the switches of [[Cell Signaling]], and this paper turned them from a list of studied enzymes into a genome-wide inventory obtained by sequence analysis. It is a model of how a gene family is mined from a genome, and its counts are the reference figures for the size of the kinome.

## How to use it

- **L2**: read the abstract for the size of the kinome and the idea of a gene family catalog.
- **L3**: use it as a case study of gene-family annotation by homology ([[Sequence Homology]], [[Gene Annotation]]).

## Caveats

- Built on the 2002 genome assembly and gene models; later annotations may change the counts.
- Verified in this pass (web search): the citation, DOI and PMID, the 518 kinase genes with the 71 new and 56 corrected ones, the 106 pseudogenes and the 244 kinases mapped to disease loci or cancer amplicons. The classification of the kinases into groups and the share of all human genes were not verified.
