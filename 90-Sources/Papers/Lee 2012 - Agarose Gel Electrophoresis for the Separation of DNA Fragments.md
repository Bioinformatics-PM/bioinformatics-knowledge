# Lee 2012 - Agarose Gel Electrophoresis for the Separation of DNA Fragments

> [!abstract]
> A peer-reviewed video protocol for separating DNA fragments by agarose gel electrophoresis, with a concise account of why DNA migrates, how the gel sieves it and what changes migration.

## Why this source

Textbooks state that small fragments run faster; this protocol adds the quantitative and practical layer a bioinformatician needs to read a gel: the working size range, the log-linear relation between distance and size, the reptation model of migration and the list of factors that shift band positions. Its abstract alone is a dense, citable summary.

## Coverage

Citation: Lee PY et al. "Agarose gel electrophoresis for the separation of DNA fragments". *J Vis Exp* (62):3923 (20 April 2012). PubMed 22546956.

## How to use it

- L1: read the abstract and watch the protocol before [[Gel Electrophoresis]] exercises.
- L2: use the list of factors to explain a gel whose bands disagree with prediction.

## Caveats

- Verified in this pass: title, journal, date, issue and article number, PubMed ID, corresponding author (Pei Yun Lee) and the abstract content summarized above. The full author list was not copied here; the video itself may require a subscription.
- "Inversely proportional to the log" is the paper's shorthand: in practice, distance decreases approximately linearly with log(size) within the resolving range.
